# Bank-Grade Box Truck Healthcare Logistics Business Plan

## 1. Executive Summary

### Business Overview

The proposed company is a Deerfield Beach, Florida-based box truck logistics operator serving ZIP 33442 and the Broward/Palm Beach corridor, with later South Florida expansion into Miami-Dade. The underwriting model assumes the company starts with one semi-new 26-foot non-CDL box truck with liftgate and focuses on healthcare-adjacent freight: DME delivery, medical supplies, healthcare replenishment, 3PL/final-mile freight, express freight, limited truck usage/rental add-ons, and storage/warehouse add-ons.

The company is modeled inside NAICS 492110, Couriers and Express Delivery Services, because that industry covers establishments primarily engaged in parcel courier and express delivery by air, surface, or combined modes, outside the universal postal service obligation ([NAICS Association](https://www.naics.com/naics-code-description/?code=492110)). The business is deliberately positioned away from generalized “load board only” trucking and toward route-density, scheduled healthcare replenishment, proof-of-delivery discipline, and higher-service healthcare-adjacent freight.

### Value Proposition

The company’s value proposition is a locally based, compliance-aware, liftgate-capable box truck service that can handle bulky healthcare-adjacent products requiring punctual delivery, documentation, careful handling, chain-of-custody discipline where required, and reliable route execution. The model favors scheduled routes and recurring B2B replenishment because those reduce empty miles, improve utilization, lower acquisition risk, and strengthen lender repayment confidence.

The business is not underwritten as a pure medical specimen courier startup because a 26-foot box truck is better suited to DME, medical equipment, medical supply replenishment, facility logistics, and palletized final-mile freight than to small specimen-only courier runs. The report assumes the company may touch PHI only where a client’s workflow makes that unavoidable, and that HIPAA obligations are handled through written policies, secure POD procedures, training, and business associate agreements when required.

### Market Opportunity

Broward and Palm Beach counties together have substantial healthcare buyer density. Census County Business Patterns reported 7,580 healthcare and social assistance establishments in Broward County and 6,933 in Palm Beach County in 2023 ([Census CBP NAICS 62](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/cbp?get=NAME,NAICS2017_LABEL,ESTAB,EMP,PAYANN&for=county:*&in=state:12&NAICS2017=62)). Within that base, ambulatory healthcare services accounted for 6,283 establishments in Broward County and 5,793 in Palm Beach County, creating a broad target universe of clinics, therapy providers, labs, outpatient centers, practices, and healthcare service locations that may require recurring deliveries ([Census CBP NAICS 621](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/cbp?get=NAME,NAICS2017_LABEL,ESTAB,EMP,PAYANN&for=county:*&in=state:12&NAICS2017=621)). Hospitals add institutional route anchors, with 31 hospital establishments in Broward County and 21 in Palm Beach County in 2023 ([Census CBP NAICS 622](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/cbp?get=NAME,NAICS2017_LABEL,ESTAB,EMP,PAYANN&for=county:*&in=state:12&NAICS2017=622)).

The logistics provider landscape is real but not prohibitive. Census County Business Patterns reported 92 courier and express delivery establishments in Broward County and 59 in Palm Beach County in 2023, which confirms market participation but also indicates that the market is fragmented enough for a specialized service provider to pursue a narrow healthcare-adjacent wedge ([Census CBP NAICS 492110](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/cbp?get=NAME,NAICS2017_LABEL,ESTAB,EMP,PAYANN&for=county:*&in=state:12&NAICS2017=492110)).

### Capital Request

The recommended base case is a semi-new box truck acquisition funded by a mix of owner equity, equipment debt, and working capital. The Excel model calculates the exact capital request dynamically from selected truck case, down payment, startup costs, insurance deposits, startup compliance spend, reserve requirements, and working capital need.

Under the base underwriting case, the recommended acquisition strategy is:

| Item | Underwriting Case | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|

| Purchase price | $66,000 estimate | Anchored to current Florida listings for 2022 International MV and comparable late-model used vehicles ([Comvoy](https://www.comvoy.com/vehicles/box-trucks/florida/davenport-1al0)) |

### Use of Funds

Use of funds includes truck down payment or lease deposit, sales tax/title/tag/fees, insurance down payment, authority/compliance registration, startup legal/accounting/entity setup, equipment and supplies, pallet jack/dollies/straps/blankets, GPS/dashcam/POD software, website/CRM/dispatch stack, initial marketing, compliance training, fuel reserve, maintenance reserve, deductible reserve, and three months of fixed-cost working capital.

### Repayment Strategy

Debt repayment is underwritten primarily from recurring route revenue, healthcare replenishment contracts, final-mile delivery contracts, and express freight. Lender repayment risk is mitigated by a model structure that prioritizes recurring customer routes, proof-of-delivery compliance, controlled DSO, fuel surcharge pass-through, customer concentration limits, and maintenance reserves.

The bank-grade target is annual DSCR of at least 1.25x by Year 2. That target is consistent with common SBA and commercial underwriting practice, where lenders frequently prefer DSCR at or above 1.25x and scrutinize repayment ability under stress ([Pioneer Capital Advisory](https://www.pioneercapitaladvisory.com/post/understanding-dscr-how-lenders-evaluate-your-deals-cash-flow)).

### Exit and Downside Strategy

The downside strategy is to preserve collateral and liquidity. If contract ramp underperforms, the company can reduce expansion spend, delay warehouse commitments, operate owner-driver longer, use rental-first capacity for overflow instead of purchasing a second vehicle, liquidate or refinance the truck, sell route contracts where assignable, or pivot to lower-compliance final-mile freight. The model includes forced liquidation value assumptions and collateral LTV to show the lender expected recovery under equipment liquidation.

## 2. Market and Industry Analysis

### TAM, SAM, SOM

The broad TAM is healthcare-adjacent local and regional logistics across South Florida, including DME, medical supplies, outpatient replenishment, interfacility logistics, final-mile freight, and time-sensitive B2B delivery. The serviceable available market is constrained to Broward and Palm Beach counties during the first operating phase, with Miami-Dade treated as an expansion market rather than a launch requirement.

The SAM is supported by official establishment density. Broward County had 7,580 healthcare and social assistance establishments in 2023, while Palm Beach County had 6,933, giving a combined two-county healthcare establishment base of 14,513 paid-employee establishments ([Census CBP NAICS 62](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/cbp?get=NAME,NAICS2017_LABEL,ESTAB,EMP,PAYANN&for=county:*&in=state:12&NAICS2017=62)). Ambulatory healthcare services represented 12,076 combined establishments across Broward and Palm Beach, indicating that most healthcare density is distributed across outpatient and clinic-like locations rather than only large hospitals ([Census CBP NAICS 621](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/cbp?get=NAME,NAICS2017_LABEL,ESTAB,EMP,PAYANN&for=county:*&in=state:12&NAICS2017=621)).

The SOM is intentionally conservative. The first-year model assumes the business captures only a small route and delivery base: 45 total loads/stops in Month 1, ramping to 190 by Month 12. That is a startup underwriting estimate, not a directly verified market statistic, and it is intentionally modest relative to the combined healthcare establishment base.

### South Florida Logistics Context

South Florida supports logistics demand through healthcare density, population density, e-commerce, port/airport connectivity, and constrained industrial land. The warehouse/storage market also confirms meaningful logistics infrastructure: Broward County had 124 warehousing and storage establishments in 2023, while Palm Beach County had 48 ([Census CBP NAICS 493](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/cbp?get=NAME,NAICS2017_LABEL,ESTAB,EMP,PAYANN&for=county:*&in=state:12&NAICS2017=493)).

Palm Beach industrial rent remains elevated but not as expensive as many Miami-Dade alternatives. Newmark reported Palm Beach industrial rent of $13.12/SF and 8.1% vacancy at Q4 2025, while also noting positive net absorption and an active construction pipeline ([Newmark Palm Beach Market Report](https://www.nmrk.com/insights/market-report/palm-beach-real-estate-market-report)). Deerfield Beach small-bay/flex asking rents are higher than the broader Palm Beach industrial benchmark, with CommercialSearch showing a Deerfield Beach average asking rent of $19.34/SF and individual listings between roughly $15 and $30/SF/YR ([CommercialSearch Deerfield Beach](https://www.commercialsearch.com/industrial/us/fl/deerfield-beach/)).

The competitive position should not be “cheapest courier.” The defensible wedge is liftgate box-truck capability, healthcare-adjacent documentation, predictable route service, real-time POD, secure handling procedures, DME/equipment setup coordination, failed-stop discipline, and B2B replenishment reliability.

### Buyer Density and Route Density Assumptions

Route density is the core operational risk variable. A single 26-foot truck can be profitable only if loaded miles, stop density, and recurring schedules reduce deadhead. The model assumes average loaded miles per load of 28, deadhead of 18%, and failed stops of 3%. Those are underwriting estimates and should be replaced with actual route data after the first 60 operating days.

Buyer density is supported by multiple local DME and medical supply prospects. BBB listings identify Total Medical Health Services in Deerfield Beach with phone 800-488-1162, Med Leaf Supply in Deerfield Beach with phone 754-212-4910, and multiple Boca Raton/Pompano/Coconut Creek medical supply companies near ZIP 33442 ([BBB Medical Supplies](https://www.bbb.org/us/fl/deerfield-beach/category/medical-supplies)). BBB DME listings identify Mobility Direct in Deerfield Beach, Care Airways in Boca Raton at 855-713-1012, Quality of Life DME Group at 888-522-7566, Right Choice Medical Supply at 561-826-8518, and Family First Medical at 754-220-8121 ([BBB Durable Medical Equipment](https://www.bbb.org/us/fl/deerfield-beach/category/durable-medical-equipment)).

### Pricing Benchmarks

Medical courier pricing varies by distance, urgency, chain-of-custody needs, vehicle type, temperature control, and route complexity. Dropoff’s 2026 guide lists standard same-day medical deliveries at $30-$45, rush deliveries at $40-$60, STAT deliveries at $90-$160+, cargo van mileage at $2.20-$3.75/mile, refrigerated vehicle mileage at $3.50-$5.25/mile, fuel surcharges of 8%-12%, and failed pickup charges of $12-$25 ([Dropoff Medical Courier Rates](https://www.dropoff.com/blog/medical-courier-service-rates/)). BearCat Express lists 2025 medical courier ranges of $35-$80 for local same-day trips, $1.50-$3.50/mile for long-distance delivery, $75-$200+ for STAT/rush trips, $500-$3,000+ per month for scheduled routes, and $20-$100 additional for special handling ([BearCat Express](https://bearcatexpress.com/medical-courier-service-rates/)).

General freight pricing provides a lower-bound benchmark but is not the recommended primary strategy. Uber Freight reported mid-December 2025 dry van average spot rates of $2.25/mile and contract rates of $2.44/mile, while refrigerated spot and contract rates were $2.62 and $2.80/mile respectively ([Uber Freight](https://www.uberfreight.com/en-US/blog/a-guide-to-freight-trucking-rates-in-2025)).

### Saturation Analysis

Broward and Palm Beach had 151 combined courier and express delivery establishments in 2023, compared with 14,513 combined healthcare and social assistance establishments ([Census CBP NAICS 492110](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/cbp?get=NAME,NAICS2017_LABEL,ESTAB,EMP,PAYANN&for=county:*&in=state:12&NAICS2017=492110), [Census CBP NAICS 62](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/cbp?get=NAME,NAICS2017_LABEL,ESTAB,EMP,PAYANN&for=county:*&in=state:12&NAICS2017=62)). That ratio does not prove unmet demand, but it supports the thesis that a specialized operator can target a narrow subset of healthcare-adjacent accounts without needing broad market share.

### Economic and Recession Sensitivity

Healthcare demand is more defensive than discretionary retail freight, but it is not immune to payment delays, reimbursement pressure, facility consolidation, insurer changes, and procurement tightening. The model therefore stress-tests downside revenue cases of 10%, 25%, and 40%, plus lost major customer, delayed receivables, insurance increase, truck breakdown, fuel spike, recession/demand contraction, and regulatory delay.

### FMCSA and DOT Assumptions

FMCSA states that companies operating commercial vehicles transporting cargo in interstate commerce must register with FMCSA and have a USDOT Number, and it identifies the relevant vehicle threshold as GVWR/GCWR/GVW/GCW of 10,001 pounds or more, whichever is greater ([FMCSA USDOT Requirements](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/registration/do-i-need-usdot-number)). FMCSA also lists Florida among states requiring intrastate commercial motor vehicle registrants to obtain a USDOT Number ([FMCSA USDOT Requirements](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/registration/do-i-need-usdot-number)).

For-hire interstate motor carriers require operating authority in addition to a USDOT Number, and FMCSA identifies OP-1 motor carrier filing with a $300 fee where operating authority is required ([FMCSA Registration Forms Guide](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/registration/registration-forms)). The model assumes the company obtains USDOT registration before launch and obtains MC authority before accepting for-hire interstate work.

### Florida Registration

Florida requires vehicles to have valid registration to operate on Florida roads, and FLHSMV states that proof of Florida insurance is required for initial registrations ([FLHSMV Motor Vehicle Registrations](https://www.flhsmv.gov/motor-vehicles-tags-titles/license-plates-registration/motor-vehicle-registrations/)). FLHSMV also identifies a $225 initial registration fee when an owner has no prior Florida plate or record to transfer ([FLHSMV Motor Vehicle Registrations](https://www.flhsmv.gov/motor-vehicles-tags-titles/license-plates-registration/motor-vehicle-registrations/)).

### CDL Assumption

The model is built around a 26-foot non-CDL truck with GVWR not exceeding 26,000 pounds. BLS defines light truck drivers as operating trucks or vans with capacity under 26,001 pounds GVW ([BLS Light Truck Drivers](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes533033.htm)). If the selected truck has GVWR of 26,001 pounds or more, the driver and operating model must be revised for CDL requirements, insurance pricing, payroll expectations, and lender risk.

### HIPAA and PHI Handling

HHS defines a business associate as a person or entity performing functions or services involving protected health information on behalf of a covered entity, and covered entities must obtain written satisfactory assurances that the business associate will safeguard PHI ([HHS HIPAA Business Associates](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html)). HHS also notes a conduit exception for the U.S. Postal Service and certain private couriers, but the safer underwriting posture is to assume written client-specific handling terms where the company receives PHI-bearing manifests, patient labels, DME setup documents, electronic POD data, or other identifiable patient information ([HHS HIPAA Business Associates](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html)).

Minimum HIPAA controls:

| Control | Required Operating Practice |
|---|---|
| PHI minimization | Drivers see only required delivery information |
| Secure POD | Password-protected mobile app, no PHI in open text when avoidable |
| Vehicle security | Lockable cab/box, no unattended visible client documents |
| Incident response | Written breach/incident escalation within 24 hours internally |
| Training | HIPAA privacy/security training at onboarding and annual refresh |
| Client agreements | BAA or courier service agreement where client requires it |

### OSHA and Healthcare-Adjacent Safety

OSHA states that employers must implement an exposure control plan where the Bloodborne Pathogens Standard applies, and the plan must describe engineering/work practice controls, PPE, employee training, medical surveillance, Hepatitis B vaccinations, and other protections required by 29 CFR 1910.1030 ([OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens](https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens)). The business should avoid regulated infectious substances, biohazard waste, sharps, and specimen transport unless it has specific client contracts, packaging procedures, DOT/IATA training where required, PPE, exposure control plan, and insurance authorization.

### Excluded and Restricted Cargo

Launch-stage restricted cargo:

| Cargo Type | Launch Policy |
|---|---|
| Regulated hazardous materials | Excluded unless DOT hazmat training, insurance, and authority are confirmed |
| Biohazard waste/sharps | Excluded unless specifically contracted and insured |
| Controlled substances | Excluded unless legal chain-of-custody and client protocol are established |
| Temperature-critical pharmaceuticals | Excluded unless validated temperature-control equipment and logs are in place |
| Patient transport | Excluded; this is freight/logistics, not NEMT |
| High-value electronics/jewelry | Excluded unless cargo limits and contract terms support it |

The recommended base case is a semi-new truck, not the cheapest used truck and not a fully new unit. The semi-new case has the best balance of lender collateral, acceptable monthly debt service, lower breakdown risk than high-mileage used inventory, and more manageable startup cash burden than new equipment.

### Accounting Treatment

The P&L separates revenue, direct variable costs, gross profit, operating expenses, EBITDA, depreciation, interest, pre-tax income, taxes, and net income. The cash flow statement uses an indirect method logic: net income plus non-cash depreciation, less working capital increases, less capital expenditures/startup investments, plus financing proceeds, less principal payments.

The balance sheet includes cash, accounts receivable, prepaid/deposits, fixed assets, accumulated depreciation, accounts payable/accruals, debt, owner equity, and retained earnings. The debt and fixed asset balances tie to the debt amortization and depreciation schedules.

### Working Capital

The base case uses DSO of 35 days and bad debt of 1.5% of revenue. Delayed receivables scenario increases DSO and shows the cash effect of healthcare and B2B billing cycles. This matters because the lender risk is not just profitability; it is timing of cash collection against fixed debt, insurance, payroll, and fuel.

## 8. Underwriting Analysis

### Core Metrics

The Excel workbook calculates:

| Metric | Formula Logic |
|---|---|
| EBITDA | Operating income plus depreciation/amortization |
| DSCR | Cash flow available for debt service divided by principal + interest |
| Gross margin | Gross profit divided by revenue |
| Net margin | Net income divided by revenue |
| Contribution margin/load | Revenue/load less variable costs/load |
| Break-even loads/month | Fixed costs divided by contribution margin/load |
| Break-even revenue/month | Break-even loads multiplied by average revenue/load |
| ROI | Cumulative net profit divided by owner equity |
| IRR | Internal rate of return on owner equity cash flows |
| NPV | Discounted owner equity cash flows at selected discount rate |
| Payback | Month when cumulative cash flow turns positive |
| ROA | Net income divided by average assets |
| ROE | Net income divided by average equity |
| Cash runway | Ending cash divided by average monthly cash burn |

### Bank Approval Tests

The underwriting approval grid is:

| Test | Target |
|---|---:|
| Base case annual DSCR by Year 2 | >= 1.25x |
| Monthly cash balance | Never below $0 |
| Customer concentration | No customer >25% of revenue |
| Fuel surcharge | Contractual or quoted for B2B work |
| Maintenance reserve | Funded monthly |
| Insurance | Bound before truck operation |
| Driver compliance | MVR/background/training complete |
| Authority | USDOT/MC as required before operations |

### Break-Even Logic

Break-even is driven by contribution margin per load. The company must cover truck debt/lease, insurance, dispatch software, storage/yard, payroll or owner labor replacement cost, marketing, accounting/legal, compliance, and reserves before generating owner-level return.

The most important lender-view break-even formula is:

\[
\text{Break-Even Loads} = \frac{\text{Monthly Fixed Costs}}{\text{Average Revenue per Load} - \text{Variable Cost per Load}}
\]

The model stress-tests break-even under lower volume, higher fuel, higher insurance, and breakdown downtime.

## 10. Collateral and Bank Risk Package

SBA 7(a) collateral guidance for 7(a) Small loans between $50,001 and $500,000 allows lenders to follow their written collateral policies for similarly sized non-SBA loans, while also stating that a loan is not to be declined solely because collateral is inadequate ([SBA 7(a) Loan Types](https://www.sba.gov/partners/lenders/7a-loan-program/types-7a-loans)).

## 16. Final Executive Underwriting Summary

### Underwriting Verdict

The strongest lender-facing case is a semi-new 26-foot non-CDL liftgate truck financed with meaningful owner equity, a three-month working capital reserve, conservative ramp assumptions, and strict compliance controls. This structure gives the owner enough operating capability to serve DME, healthcare replenishment, medical supplies, and final-mile freight without forcing new-truck debt service before contracts are proven.

### What Must Be True

The business works only if the owner executes route density. The company must convert local healthcare-adjacent buyers into recurring routes, keep customer concentration below 25%, pass fuel increases through with surcharge language, maintain proof-of-delivery compliance above 95%, keep deadhead below 25%, and maintain cash reserves through ramp.

### What Would Break the Deal

The deal becomes weak if the owner chooses a high-mileage truck without repair reserve, signs a warehouse lease before demand is proven, accepts low-margin spot freight without surcharge, ignores receivable timing, allows one customer to dominate revenue, fails to bind insurance before operation, or handles restricted medical cargo without written procedures and coverage.
